# K2: revert burn, the failed-transaction index, and loop burn

Run: 2026-10-07, real Monad **mainnet** (chain 143), read-only. No mocks, no synthetic rows.

- Code: `packages/bench/src/failed-index.ts` (index + `RunTracker` + trace/re-sim layer), `scripts/k2-sample.ts`.
- Data: `results/k2-sample.json` — `.full` (exact 30-day census), `.sample` (200-window stratified estimate with CIs), `.traces` (trace sub-sample).
- Cache: `.readset/cache/failed/sqd/*.jsonl.gz` (one file per 12 000-block grid chunk, resumable) and `.readset/cache/traces/k2-traces.jsonl`.

## Verdict: **PASS on the burn half of K2**

K2 passes if *either* "knowable-at-N−3 is ≥25% of burned MON" *or* "loop burn is a meaningful share". The knowable-at-N−k replay is a separate work item; this document settles the denominator and the loop term.

| K2 clause (this work item) | Result |
|---|---|
| A 30-day failed-transaction index exists, from real mainnet | **PASS** — exact census of 8 568 000 blocks, 6 257 528 failed txs |
| Revert burn is material | **PASS** — **1 161 443 MON** in 30 days, 38 715 MON/day |
| Burn is concentrated enough to target | **PASS** — top 50 senders = **69.7%**, top 1 000 = 92.0% |
| Loop burn is a meaningful share | **PASS, but it is the smaller term** — runs of ≥10 consecutive failures = **73 556 MON (6.33%)**; a breaker of 5 would have stopped **53 592 MON (4.6%)** |
| Padded gas limits are common (hardcoded-gas proxy) | **PASS** — in 600 traced failures, gas limit ≥2× re-simulated gas on 33.9% of txs carrying **63.2% of burn** (95% CI 49.2–72.6); 47.8% of all gas bought by failed txs was never needed |
| Out-of-gas share | 9.5% of failures (1.11% of burn), matching Category Labs' 0.58%-of-all-txs once rebased |

So the headline is **not** loops. Loops are real (6.33% of burn, 2 452 MON/day, 4 672 distinct senders) and they are the demoable part, but 93.7% of the burn is one-off failures spread over 65 316 senders. The pitch must lead with the burn and the concentration, and use loops as the breaker's proof, not as the size of the market.

The strongest number here is the one the PRD treated as a side quest: **nearly half the gas bought by failed transactions was never going to be used** (47.8%), and limits of ≥2× what the transaction consumed account for 63.2% of the burn. That is the gate's and the buffer's territory, and it does not depend on the knowable-at-N−k replay landing.

## Loop burn

A "loop" is a run of **≥10 consecutive failed nonces from one sender**. Every mined transaction consumes a nonce, so contiguous failed nonces prove no success in between — no pending feed needed (`RunTracker` in `failed-index.ts`).

|---|---|
| Runs of ≥10 | 19 999 |
| Failed txs inside those runs | 486 551 (7.8% of all failures) |

The top two are the demo: two addresses hitting the same selector, 6 107 and 5 874 failures each, overlapping and ending in the same block (108 331 128) — about 3.6 hours of wall clock at 0.4 s blocks. A breaker of 5 would have cut each to 5 failures. That is the "agent stuck in a loop at 3am" slide, and it is a real pair of mainnet addresses, not a mock.

The stratified sample, whose 1 h windows clip every run, estimates loop burn at 50 491 MON (95% CI 35 683–69 605) — visibly below the census's 73 556, which is the expected direction and a useful consistency check on the sampler.

## Hardcoded-gas proxy and out-of-gas share

PRD definition: failed transactions whose gas limit is **≥2× the re-simulated gas**. We cannot observe hardcoding, only padding.

Getting the re-simulated number needed a detour, because **Monad's full-limit charge leaks into the traces too**: `debug_traceTransaction` with `callTracer` reports `gasUsed == gas == gasLimit` for the top-level call on every failed tx we checked. The trace cannot tell you how much gas the transaction actually needed. What the trace *does* give is the top-level `error` field, which is how out-of-gas is separated from a logic revert.

So `resimGas()` (in `failed-index.ts`) re-simulates at the **parent block**, two ways:

1. **`eth_estimateGas` at N−1.** Answers when the transaction would have succeeded against the parent state. Verified against `eth_call` at several gas allowances on real txs.
2. **Bisection with `eth_call` at N−1.** When the tx reverts at the parent block, `eth_estimateGas` refuses. Monad answers a starved call with `-32003 "out of gas"` (and `"intrinsic gas greater than limit"` below the intrinsic floor) and a revert otherwise, so "did this allowance run out of gas?" is an observable predicate and the gas-to-revert can be bisected. Tolerance 1%, so the result is an **upper** bound on gas consumed and the reported `gasLimit / resim` ratio is slightly **understated**.

Endpoints: `rpc2.monad.xyz` (Goldsky) and `rpc-mainnet.monadinfra.com`, the only two free mainnet archive nodes with tracing per K1, paced to ~8 rps combined with per-endpoint cooldown and failover (`ArchivePacer`).

### Results, 600 traced failed txs

600 failed transactions, drawn uniformly inside 60 stratified 1 h windows over blocks `102 756 000`–`111 323 999` (the same 30-day span as the census, ending ~96 000 blocks later because the trace run resolved its own head). 4 282 RPC calls, 0 failures. Shares of burn are ratio estimates weighted by each row's `burnWei`; intervals are 95% cluster bootstraps over the 60 windows.

| Measure | Of failed txs | Of burned MON |
|---|---|---|
| **Out of gas** | **9.5%** (CI 6.0–14.2) | **1.11%** (CI 0.55–2.03) |
| **Gas limit ≥2× re-simulated gas** | **33.9%** (CI 29.5–38.3) | **63.2%** (CI 49.2–72.6) |
| Gas limit ≥3× | 28.5% | 48.8% |
| Gas limit ≥10× | 15.6% | 14.8% |

Resolved to a gas number: 572 of 600. The 28 unresolved are 25 that ran out of gas at their own limit against the parent state (genuinely under-funded, the opposite of padded) and 3 the endpoints would not answer.

`gasLimit / resim` distribution: p10 1.01×, p25 1.10×, **p50 1.56×**, p75 4.04×, p90 19.5×, p99 42.8×, max **121.6×**.

**Gas actually needed was 52.2% of gas paid for** — i.e. **47.8% of the gas bought by failed transactions was never going to be used**, and on Monad it was paid for anyway. Scaled naively onto the census that is ~555 000 MON of the 1.16M, though the trace sample is 600 rows and the interval on it is wide, so quote the share, not the scaled MON.

Note the shape: ≥2× covers 63.2% of burn but ≥10× only 14.8%. The money is in the 2–10× band — ordinary 2–4× padding on high-gas calls — not in the spectacular 100× outliers. The `0.58%` out-of-gas figure the PRD quotes from Category Labs is *of all transactions*; as a share *of failures* that is ~9.7%, and we measure 9.5%. Two independent samples, different windows, same number: good corroboration for both.

**Two trace-error findings worth keeping:**

- `reserve balance violation` appears as a top-level trace error (3 of 600). This is Monad's reserve-balance rule showing up as a failure mode in its own right. The PRD already says reserve-balance reverts must be excluded from "avoidable" — this is the string to filter on, and the gate's reserve check (Component 1) has a measurable job.
- 2 of 600 returned a bare `"error"` as the trace error. Unexplained; too rare to matter for the numbers, but do not assume the error string is always meaningful.

### Caveat on the proxy

This measures **padding, not hardcoding**. A 2× limit can come from a hardcoded constant, from a wallet's default when estimation reverts (the Monad gas-pricing docs call out MetaMask doing exactly this), or from a deliberately generous buffer. All three are the same loss to the sender, which is what the breaker and the gate act on, but the deck must not claim 63% of burn comes from hardcoded gas. It comes from limits at least twice what the transaction would have consumed.

Also, the re-simulation is at **N−1**, not at the sender's actual send time, and the gas a transaction consumes depends on the state it runs against. For the 340 txs that succeed at N−1 the estimate is exactly what a wallet would have computed; for the 232 that revert there, the bisected gas-to-revert can be *lower* than the gas the successful path would have needed, which inflates their ratio. That is the main reason to read ≥2× as a proxy with a wide interval rather than a measurement.

### Side observation: replay at the parent block

Not the K2 knowable-share benchmark — that is a separate work item and uses N−1/N−2/N−3 with the reserve-balance exclusions. But it falls out of the same calls, so: of 600 failed txs replayed against the **parent** block's state, 340 (56.7%) still succeeded, 232 (38.7%) already reverted, 25 ran out of gas at their own limit, 3 unknown. The 232 that already reverted at N−1 carry **50.1% of the sampled burn**. Treat that as an encouraging smoke signal for the N−1 number, not as the result: it is a 600-row sample, it uses `eth_call` rather than the full dependency-set replay, and it applies none of the exclusions.

## Method, and what would make these numbers wrong

**Source.** The bulk index streams the SQD Portal public dataset `monad-mainnet` (`portal.sqd.dev`), keyless, which carries per-transaction `status`, `gas`, `gasUsed`, `effectiveGasPrice`, `nonce`, `sighash` and `transactionIndex`. It is a mirror of mainnet, not a second chain, and `fetchFailedTxs({source:'rpc'})` implements the same index over `eth_getBlockByNumber(full)` + `eth_getBlockReceipts`. The test suite cross-validates the two on the same blocks and asserts identical hashes, `burnWei`, `from` and `selector` — that assertion is the licence to use the fast source for 8.57M blocks. The pure-RPC path costs ~170 KB and two calls per block against a 25 rps cap, so a 30-day window is ≥8 days of wall clock and ~1.5 TB of transfer before retries; it is kept for spot checks and for anyone who wants to re-derive a range from the node itself.

**System transactions.** Monad puts a gas-0 system transaction at block index 0 (staking/reward syscall to the `0x…1000` precompile). It is excluded from every denominator and can never be a failed row: the filter is `gas == 0`, which is stricter than matching the address and also catches any other zero-gas syscall. 8 568 342 of 176 161 849 txs were dropped this way, 1.00004 per block, consistent with exactly one per block plus 342 extras.

**Known weaknesses.**

- `effectiveGasPrice` comes from the receipt, so the burn is in MON. No USD figure is quoted anywhere; do not convert on stage without a dated price source.
- The 30-day window ends at a finalized grid boundary, so "30 days" is 8 568 000 blocks, not a wall-clock month. Block time drift would stretch or shrink the real span.
- Loop detection assumes nonces are strictly increasing per sender within the window and that a nonce gap means a success. A nonce gap could instead be a tx that never landed; that would make the loop numbers a slight over-count, bounded by how often that happens.
- Top-50 share in the stratified sample picks its top 50 *inside* the sample, which biases that statistic upward; the census number (69.7%) is the one to quote, and the sample's 70.9% is only there as a cross-check.
- The trace sub-sample is small by design (traces are slow). Its confidence intervals are cluster-bootstrapped over the sampled 1 h windows, because rows inside one window are frequently the same bot and treating them as independent would understate the interval.
- A 1% bisection tolerance and a 30M gas ceiling bound the re-simulated gas; both are recorded in the output.
